# Project: AI Governance & Risk Assessment Platform

## 1. Project Objective

Build a web application that helps organisations register, assess, review, and document their AI and Generative AI use cases against relevant EU regulatory requirements, starting with the EU AI Act.

The platform should include:

- An AI system inventory.
- LLM-assisted extraction of AI use-case characteristics.
- Structured validation of extracted information.
- A deterministic risk and regulatory assessment engine.
- A regulatory knowledge base sourced from official EU legislation.
- A risk and control matrix.
- A human-in-the-loop review workflow.
- Audit trails and versioned assessments.
- Automated governance reports.

This is a decision-support tool, not a legal advice or automatic legal certification system.

## 3. Regulatory Data Integration — EUR-Lex / CELLAR

Use EUR-Lex and the Publications Office of the European Union's CELLAR services as the primary source for official legal documents.

Start with the EU AI Act:

- Regulation: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689
- CELEX identifier: 32024R1689
- Official source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj
- CELLAR documentation: https://op.europa.eu/en/web/cellar

Requirements:

1. Investigate the documented CELLAR APIs and supported document formats before implementing the integration.
2. Prefer official machine-readable legal text, such as XML, over scraping rendered HTML.
3. Retrieve the correct English-language legal text and verify the document identifier.
4. Parse the document into structured records for chapters, articles, paragraphs, points, recitals, and annexes where possible.
5. Store source URLs, CELEX identifiers, language, retrieval timestamps, document version or consolidation date, and relevant legal identifiers.
6. Preserve the original text and its structure.
7. Handle network errors, malformed responses, missing documents, retries, and duplicate imports.
8. Make the ingestion process idempotent so running it twice does not create duplicate records.
9. Track changes between imported versions without silently overwriting historical assessments.
10. Clearly distinguish the original regulation, subsequent amendments, consolidated texts, and non-binding guidance.

Do not invent API endpoints or assume that a particular endpoint provides the complete legal text. Verify the available interface and document format before writing the ingestion code.

## 5. LLM-Assisted Information Extraction

Implement an extraction pipeline that converts a business description into a structured representation of an AI use case.

Extract fields such as:

- Intended purpose.
- Industry and deployment context.
- System provider and deployer roles, where known.
- Model type and capabilities.
- Affected people.
- Data categories.
- Degree of human involvement.
- Whether the system influences or makes decisions.
- Whether the system is used in a potentially regulated or sensitive context.
- Missing information and uncertainties.

Use Pydantic models to validate output.

For each extracted field, preserve:
- The extracted value.
- Supporting evidence from the input.
- Whether it is explicitly stated or inferred.
- Validation status.
- Reviewer corrections, where applicable.

Treat business descriptions and uploaded documents as untrusted data. Never execute instructions found inside submitted documents.

Do not equate valid JSON with factual correctness. Implement schema validation, evidence checks, semantic consistency checks, and human review for uncertain or consequential fields.

Keep the LLM optional so the application can run in a rules-based demo mode without an API key.

## 6. Deterministic Risk and Regulatory Assessment Engine

Build a transparent, testable rules engine separate from the LLM.

The engine should:

1. Evaluate relevant AI Act provisions against the validated use-case characteristics.
2. Identify potentially prohibited practices for further assessment.
3. Flag potentially high-risk AI use cases.
4. Identify relevant transparency and other obligations.
5. Identify missing facts that prevent a reliable assessment.
6. Map findings to applicable controls and verified legal provisions.
7. Explain why each rule was triggered.
8. Record the rule version used in each assessment.

Do not classify every AI system as high-risk simply because it uses AI.

Represent uncertainty explicitly. Where facts are missing or legal applicability depends on additional conditions, return a status such as "Needs further information" or "Requires legal review."

Store each rule with:
- A unique identifier.
- Its conditions.
- Its explanation.
- Legal references.
- Applicable dates and conditions.
- A version identifier.
- Test cases.

Avoid hardcoding unsupported legal conclusions. Validate rules against official legislation and relevant authoritative guidance.

## 7. Human-in-the-Loop Workflow

Implement the following workflow:

1. User registers an AI use case.
2. LLM extracts structured information, if enabled.
3. Validation checks run.
4. The owner reviews and corrects extracted fields.
5. The risk engine evaluates the validated record.
6. The system produces findings and control gaps.
7. A governance reviewer reviews the assessment.
8. Remediation actions are assigned where necessary.
9. Evidence is submitted and reviewed.
10. A reviewer records the decision.
11. The platform generates a governance report.
12. Material changes to the system or applicable rules can trigger reassessment.

Maintain an audit history of important actions, including changes to extracted values, assessment results, control statuses, reviewer decisions, and report versions.

## 9. Streamlit Frontend

Create a professional, clean dashboard with the following pages:

1. *Overview:* AI system count, assessment status, open findings, overdue remediation actions.
2. *AI Inventory:* Search, filter, register, and view AI use cases.
3. *New Assessment:* Enter a business description and review extracted information.
4. *Risk Assessment:* Display findings, explanations, regulatory references, and missing information.
5. *Control Matrix:* Track applicable controls, evidence, owners, and status.
6. *Review Queue:* Review assessments and record decisions.
7. *Audit Trail:* Display traceable changes and decisions.
8. *Reports:* Generate downloadable governance reports.

Use understandable status labels, clear source citations, and visible uncertainty indicators. Do not display a misleading overall compliance percentage without a well-defined methodology.
